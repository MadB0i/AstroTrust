\begin{abstract}
Textual disclaimers alone fail to capture the safety risks of consumer AI astrology apps. Under AstroTrust~2.0, we audit conversational outputs alongside checkout flows, network traffic, and compiled Android binaries across $N=93$ sessions: 27 frontier web captures (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), 6 live sessions across 5 Indian apps (\texttt{Astrotalk}, \texttt{Pandit Ji}, \texttt{Reva}, \texttt{AstroSage}, \texttt{AstroTime}), and 60 balanced local runs (\texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B} [$N=30$], \texttt{1.5B} [$N=30$]). Frontier models preserve firm epistemic boundaries (27/27) with zero monetization hooks. Unmonetized local runs exhibit high epistemic instability (23/30 [76.7\%] at 0.5B; 16/30 [53.3\%] at 1.5B; Fisher $p = 1.71 \times 10^{-9}$ vs.\ frontier), though scaling to 1.5B cuts explicit zodiac drift from 19/30 to 8/30 ($p = 0.0089$) and belief-conditioned instability from 19/20 to 9/20 ($p = 0.0012$). Conversely, 3/6 consumer sessions emit calibrated text while 6/6 (100\%, $p = 1.31 \times 10^{-9}$ vs.\ 87 unmonetized runs) deploy CCPA~2023 dark patterns: a INR~1 trial binding a 5-year INR~699 UPI Autopay mandate (\texttt{Reva}), INR~5 to 19/min surges stranding INR~15 balances (\texttt{Astrotalk}), and shared 16s audio under mismatched avatars (\texttt{Astrotalk}/\texttt{Pandit Ji}). Five-APK forensics shows \texttt{Astrotalk v1.1.545} mirrors all 787 entries of \texttt{assets/apppackges.json}~[sic] into manifest \texttt{<queries>} (787/787; 804 unique) without \texttt{QUERY\_ALL\_PACKAGES} and targets \texttt{user/apps/installed}, whereas sister clone \texttt{Pandit Ji} (6,193 shared paths) has 26 queries and zero tracker. Android~14 MITM capture confirms guest \texttt{POST save/user-upi-apps}. On a non-circular $N=44$ unseen benchmark, pre-locked \texttt{Rules-v2} (F1 = 0.700, \texttt{TN=5}) beats memorized \texttt{Rules-v1} (F1 = 0.000) and avoids dual-pole collapse in naive (F1 = 0.000) and oversampled (\texttt{TN=0}) 0.5B LoRA judges.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Millions of Indian smartphone users now open horoscope and Kundali applications when they face uncertain decisions about employment, debt, marriage, and medical care. Major platforms have replaced long queues for human astrologers with conversational bots marketed as instant ``AI astrologers'', mixing ephemeris math, streaming chat completions, and synthetic voice notes.

Standard AI safety benchmarks test whether a model hallucinates facts or flatters false user assumptions inside an isolated text box. That setup breaks down when applied to paid consumer apps. If an app prints a cautious paragraph telling an applicant to focus on interview preparation, text evaluators mark the model safe. What those evaluators never see is the INR~1 checkout screen that just registered a five-year UPI Autopay mandate, the 16-second voice note stalling the answer behind a INR~19/min paywall, or the background routine scanning the user's phone for loan, gambling, fertility, and dating apps.

This paper brings those hidden software layers into the same audit frame as model completions. We evaluate four model cohorts in parallel: web frontier chatbots ($N=27$), live Indian commercial apps ($N=6$), and balanced local open-weight baselines across two parameter scales (\texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B} $N=30$, \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B} $N=30$), alongside static disassembly of five valid APKs and live Android~14 MITM proxy captures. Every claim implied by our title maps to a concrete measurement (Table~\ref{tab:proofmap}): epistemic reliance tiers and belief-conditioned shifts across matched prompts, logged commercial dark patterns cross-referenced against Indian consumer law, and binary- plus network-level evidence of device profiling.

We make four grounded contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Multi-Scale Tripartite Empirical Dataset ($N=93$):} We collect cryptographically hashed responses across frontier web models ($N=27$), paid and trial Indian consumer apps ($N=6$), and balanced local inference runs across two Qwen2.5 scales ($0.5$B $N=30$, $1.5$B $N=30$) under shared scenarios and belief frames.
\item \textbf{Dark-Pattern Mapping to Indian Law:} We document six live consumer sessions (\texttt{consumer-1.1} schema) and tie each UI, audio, pricing, and checkout tactic directly to the 2023 guidelines issued by India's Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA).
\item \textbf{Five-APK Forensics and Android~14 Proxy Capture:} We statically disassemble all five validated Indian astrology APKs (including 76.6~MB \texttt{AstroSage AI v32.6}), contrast \texttt{Astrotalk}'s 787-app \texttt{<queries>} harvesting against sister app \texttt{Pandit Ji}, and intercept live Android~14 traffic confirming guest \texttt{POST save/user-upi-apps} telemetry.
\item \textbf{Non-Circular Dual-Layer Auditor and Dual-Pole Collapse Analysis:} We release pre-locked \texttt{AstroGuard Rules-v2} alongside naive and minority-oversampled 0.5B LoRA adapters (\S\ref{sec:guard}), demonstrating on $N=44$ strictly unseen traces how \texttt{Rules-v2} preserves discrimination (F1 = 0.700, \texttt{TN=5}) where memorized \texttt{Rules-v1} fails (F1 = 0.000) and small LoRA adapters suffer dual-pole collapse.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Indian Regulatory Context}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Astrology and Personal Validation.}
Empirical scrutiny of natal chart matching goes back decades. \citet{carlson1985} ran a double-blind test designed with professional astrological bodies and found astrologers matched birth charts to California Psychological Inventory profiles at 34\% accuracy against a 33.3\% random baseline, even when claiming peak confidence. Earlier, \citet{forer1949} handed college students identical personality sketches stitched together from horoscope columns; students rated the vague text 4.26 out of 5 for personal accuracy. Automated horoscope chat turns this Barnum-Forer dynamic into a per-minute retention loop.

\paragraph{LLM Sycophancy and Preference Bias.}
Modern alignment pipelines risk reproducing that flattery for different reasons. \citet{perez2023} observed that larger RLHF-trained models increasingly echo a user's stated political or factual premise. \citet{sharma2024} traced this agreement bias back to human annotator preferences embedded inside reward models: when a user signals a desired answer, models often abandon neutral grounding to validate it. In India, where cultural openness to astrology meets high-stakes career or loan anxiety, sycophantic validation carries direct financial costs.

\paragraph{Dark Patterns and India's CCPA 2023 Framework.}
\citet{gray2018} grouped manipulative interfaces into five strategies: nagging, obstruction, sneaking, interface interference, and forced action. Auditing roughly 11,000 e-commerce storefronts, \citet{mathur2019} cataloged 1,818 dark-pattern deployments including countdown urgency and hidden subscriptions. On November 30, 2023, India's Central Consumer Protection Authority codified 13 actionable dark patterns under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019~\citep{ccpa2023}, expressly covering \emph{Subscription Traps}, \emph{Drip Pricing}, \emph{Bait and Switch}, \emph{False Urgency}, \emph{Interface Interference}, and \emph{Disguised Advertisements}.

\paragraph{Android Package Visibility and Installed-App Profiling.}
A static list of apps installed on a phone reveals remarkably intimate traits. \citet{seneviratne2014} showed that an app-installation snapshot predicts user gender with roughly 70\% accuracy alongside inferred income and lifestyle proxies. Starting with Android~11 (API level~30), Google restricted \texttt{PackageManager.getInstalledPackages()} behind package visibility filtering~\citep{androidqueries2020}. Developers wanting broad visibility must seek Google Play Console approval for the restricted \texttt{QUERY\_ALL\_PACKAGES} permission, normally limited to file managers or antivirus software, or declare explicit targets inside \texttt{AndroidManifest.xml} \texttt{<queries>} tags. In parallel, the Reserve Bank of India's Digital Lending Guidelines~\citep{rbi2022} barred regulated lending apps from harvesting installed-app lists or contact books. Consumer astrology apps sit outside RBI supervision yet inspect overlapping financial and debt telemetry, straining the purpose-limitation and data-minimization mandates of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023~\citep{dpdp2023}, which restricts consented personal data processing to what is strictly necessary for a specified service purpose.
