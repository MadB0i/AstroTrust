\subsection{Cross-Domain Instability and Scale Effects in \texttt{Qwen2.5} (\texorpdfstring{$N=60$}{N=60})}
Expanding local inference on the GTX~1650 ($T=0.7$, top-$p=0.9$, max 512 tokens) across all 8 Indian domains and 3 belief frames at both \texttt{0.5B} ($N=30$) and \texttt{1.5B} ($N=30$) uncovers two distinct failure mechanisms (Table~\ref{tab:domains}). At \texttt{0.5B}, introducing any user astrological expectation surges epistemic instability from \textbf{40.0\%} (4/10) under neutral framing to \textbf{95.0\%} (19/20; 10/10 positive, 9/10 negative; two-sided Fisher $p=0.0021$). The \texttt{0.5B} model hallucinates constellations as Moon signs (\emph{``Ursa Major (Sagittarius)''}) while advising graduate applicants to prioritize Moon signs over academic records (\texttt{atb-education-002}), treats Mars and Saturn as zodiac signs predicting salary gains (\texttt{atb-relocation-006}), invents clinical triage deadlines (\texttt{atb-health-005}), and tells symptomatic medical patients to maintain regular astrologer appointments.

Scaling 3$\times$ to \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} sharply reduces expectation-triggered destabilization from 95.0\% (19/20) to \textbf{45.0\%} (9/20, Fisher $p=0.0012$), cuts explicit drift/hallucinations by more than half from 63.3\% to \textbf{26.7\%} ($p=0.0089$), and cuts mid-sentence truncation from 46.7\% to 20.0\%. Yet \texttt{1.5B} still registers \textbf{53.3\%} (16/30) overall instability because polite disclaimers mask subtle guidance drift: it still advises small-business loan applicants to hire astrologers and claims Jupiter/Venus alignment reduces loan interest rates (\texttt{atb-finance-004}, 3/3 unsafe), urges job-offer movers to seek accurate predictions from astrologers (\texttt{atb-relocation-006}, 3/3 unsafe), prescribes planetary date-avoidance for family gatherings (\texttt{atb-family-007}, 3/3 unsafe), and shifts Aquarius/Pisces calendar bounds into Aries/Taurus dates (\texttt{atb-career-001}).

\section{Dual-Layer Safeguards and Honest LoRA Limits}
\label{sec:guard}

Because \texttt{Reva} writes calm reflective prose on top of a five-year autopay trap and \texttt{Astrotalk} reads 787 installed packages off the phone before chat billing even starts, conversational moderation needs two distinct layers: \textbf{Layer~1 (\texttt{AstroGuard})} inspects prompt-response text for dark-pattern phrasing, elevated ADRS tiers, and hallucinations; \textbf{Layer~2 (\texttt{AstroAudit})} audits APK manifests, bundled assets, DEX upload routes, and billing screens.

For Layer~1, we compare deterministic rule baselines against compact LoRA adapters~\citep{hu2022} fine-tuned on \texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct} under a strictly \textbf{non-circular out-of-domain and scale-transfer benchmark}. After fixing blank-field trace extraction across the initial 49-run dev corpus, we formed stratified splits (\texttt{39 train}: 4 consumer, 22 frontier, 13 local; \texttt{10 val}: 2 consumer, 5 frontier, 3 local) and trained two prompt-masked LoRA adapters ($r=8$, $\alpha=16$, \texttt{q\_proj}/\texttt{v\_proj}, 540,672 trainable parameters or 0.1093\%, 4 epochs on GTX~1650): (i) \textbf{LoRA-0.5B naive} on the raw 39-row dev split (40 steps, 622.8s, final loss 0.5246), and (ii) \textbf{LoRA-0.5B balanced}, which oversamples minority unsafe/hybrid examples strictly inside \texttt{train.jsonl} (64 rows: 28 \texttt{SAFE\_CALIBRATED}, 36 unsafe/hybrid; 64 steps, 1031.7s, final loss 0.5644). To eliminate rule-tuning leakage, we locked domain-general \textbf{Rules-v2} heuristics \emph{before} generating the 44 new cross-domain (\texttt{0.5B}) and \texttt{1.5B} completions, and manually audited all 44 unseen completions with verbatim evidence quotes into \texttt{heldout\_unseen\_labeled.jsonl} (15 \texttt{SAFE\_CALIBRATED}, 29 \texttt{UNSAFE\_EPISTEMIC\_DRIFT}). Table~\ref{tab:guard} reports all evaluators across this $N=44$ unseen set and the $N=10$ in-domain validation split (\texttt{val10}).

Testing deterministic rules and small LoRA adapters side by side on unseen splits exposes four sharp lessons:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Memorized Rules Collapse Out-of-Domain:} \texttt{Rules-v1} rules tuned tightly to the initial 49 runs score 0.000 unsafe F1 and 0.000 drift F1 on the 44 unseen completions (\texttt{TP=0, FN=29}), whereas pre-locked domain-general \texttt{Rules-v2} preserves 0.700 unsafe F1 (\texttt{P=0.677, R=0.724, TN=5}) and 0.750 precision on explicit drift (\texttt{F1=0.261}).
\item \textbf{Dual-Pole Collapse in Small LoRA Adapters:} On unweighted training data (71.8\% safe, 28/39), \texttt{LoRA-0.5B naive} collapses to predicting \texttt{SAFE\_CALIBRATED} for 10/10 \texttt{val10} and 44/44 held-out traces (unsafe F1 = 0.000, missing \texttt{consumer-e8678302} and \texttt{consumer-cf90d7dc}; drift F1 = 0.323 on held-out due to JSON field contradictions). Oversampling unsafe training rows flips \texttt{LoRA-0.5B balanced} to the \emph{opposite pole}: it flags 43/44 held-out runs (\texttt{TP=28, FP=15, FN=1, TN=0}) and 10/10 \texttt{val10} traces (\texttt{TN=0}) as unsafe, inflating F1 to 0.778 without true discrimination (drift precision 0.419 vs.\ 0.750 for \texttt{Rules-v2}).
\item \textbf{Unseen Multilingual Cold Reading:} The held-out \texttt{AstroTime} sample triggered Hinglish flattery phrases unseen in the three training consumer apps, confirming that $N=4$ consumer training traces cannot cover Hindi/Hinglish cold-reading idioms without multilingual augmentation.
\item \textbf{Architecture Implication:} Until larger cross-lingual human-annotated corpora are collected, pre-locked domain-general lexical rules (\texttt{Rules-v2}) paired with Layer-2 manifest, asset, DEX, and wire-traffic inspection (\texttt{AstroAudit}) remain indispensable over standalone 0.5B neural judges.
\end{enumerate}
